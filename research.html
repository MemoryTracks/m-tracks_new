Research

Research first. Product second.

Every Memory Tracks product starts as a research question, tested with the people who will use it and published for others to examine.

Our approach

  • Song-task association

    Music heard in childhood and adolescence is among the most durable of human memories. We link a personally meaningful song to a specific task, so that hearing the song becomes the cue to act.

  • Tested in real settings

    We work with care homes, GP practices and NHS teams, not in the lab alone.

  • Published, including the limits

    We report what worked, what did not, and what needs a larger study.

Published research

2026 · MedTracks · NHS Highland · Under review

Utilising Memorable Music via a Smartphone Application to Support Medication Adherence: A Mixed-Methods Feasibility Study

Brill M, Anderson G, Gibson A, Whalley JH. Submitted to Digital Health.

31 adults aged 40 and over (median age 69), prescribed medication for conditions linked to a higher risk of dementia, used MedTracks for 30 days. They were recruited through three GP practices in Inverness, working with NHS Highland's Clinical Research Facility, between December 2024 and February 2025.

  • Forgetting fell significantlyWilcoxon p = .014, Cohen's d = 0.633. 40% of participants improved by one category.
  • 81% saw remindersevery time or most of the time
  • 77% positiveof interview comments about music as a reminder

The authors describe it as the first study to test music as a cue for taking medication. They found the approach feasible and acceptable, and recommend a larger controlled trial with objective measures of adherence.

Partners: NHS Highland, University for the Creative Arts. Funded by Innovate UK through the Small Business Research Initiative (SBRI). Ethics: IRAS 347639.

2019 · Memory Tracks · UK care homes · Open access

Assessing Wellbeing in People Living with Dementia Using Reminiscence Music with a Mobile App (Memory Tracks): A Mixed Methods Cohort Study

Cunningham S, Brill M, Whalley JH, Read R, Anderson G, Edwards S, Picking R. Journal of Healthcare Engineering 2019; 8924273.

An exploratory study with 14 people living with dementia in UK care homes, measuring wellbeing daily before and during use of the Memory Tracks app. The quantitative measures showed no significant change in this small sample. Care staff reported positive changes in residents' behaviour, ability and routine. The study shaped the method we later applied to medication.

Learning from the trial

What the trial taught us

Most participants found the app manageable: 75% did not think they would need technical support. The overall usability score was 58.5 out of 100, which the authors link largely to installing a test version outside the app stores. Participants also missed some features, including snooze.

  • MedTracks is now available from the App Store and Google Play.
  • Our help guides show how to mark a dose as taken or snooze it straight from the notification.
  • The guides also cover set-up and the phone settings that keep reminders reliable.
